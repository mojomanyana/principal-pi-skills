## Review

**Verdict:** Over-engineered. Remove it.

The `GreeterFactory` abstraction earns its keep only when:
- There are **multiple** `kind` values at call sites, or
- `HelloGreeter` instantiation is complex enough to justify hiding behind a factory.

Neither is true here. The single branch maps 1:1 to the single concrete class, and every caller passes `'hello'`.

**What to do:** Replace `GreeterFactory().create('hello')` with `HelloGreeter()` at every call site, then delete the class.

This simplifies in three ways:
1. Removes a dead conditional branch (`if kind == 'hello'`).
2. Eliminates the stringly-typed dispatch (no typo risk on `'hello'`).
3. Removes an indirection that obscures what's actually being constructed.

If a second greeter arrives later, a factory (or a simple mapping dict) can be introduced then — but only when it's actually needed.
